1.0 Kit Overview

Weather in Rhythm is a hybrid digital and physical Songs and Poems Resource Kit designed for Year 4 ESL pupils. The kit combines weather vocabulary, movement, singing, poetry reading, rhyme recognition, creative writing and digital drawing.

The digital website is organised into three connected activities: Move with the Weather!, Weather Word Hunt, and Draw It, Write It! Studio. The activities progress from language input and guided practice to creative production.

Lesson progression:
Listen → Speak → Sing → Move → Read → Notice → Rhyme → Write → Draw → Recite

Learning Outcomes

By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

  1. Identify and pronounce four core weather words: sunny, rainy, windy and cloudy.
  2. Identify key vocabulary and recognise the rhyming pair tree–sea in Robert Louis Stevenson’s poem Rain.
  3. Create and present a simple four-line weather poem using suitable descriptive language.

3.0 Before-Class Preparation

  1. Open the Weather in Rhythm website on the teacher’s laptop or classroom computer.
  2. Connect the device to the projector or interactive display.
  3. Check the classroom speakers and adjust the BGM volume to a comfortable level.
  4. Use Test Voice to confirm that speech synthesis is working.
  5. Open Teacher Zone and select an available English voice if needed.
  6. Test the weather cards, poem recital, rhyme challenge and drawing tools before the lesson.
  7. Prepare the physical companion materials and print enough worksheets for the class.
  8. Keep a printed poem and poem-writing worksheet available as a backup if a digital device is unavailable.
Important: Browser voice options vary by computer and operating system. The teacher should test the selected voice on the actual classroom device before pupils arrive.

5.0 Lesson Architecture & Implementation

🎵
Activity 1

5.1 Move with the Weather!

Focus: Listening & Speaking

Target words: sunny, rainy, windy, cloudy

Step 1 — Meet the Weather Friends: Pupils tap or flip the four digital weather cards. They listen to the pronunciation, read the example sentence and notice the movement cue on the back of each card.
Step 2 — Practise the Actions: The teacher models the four TPR actions: touch the sky, hands down low, sway around and turn around. Physical action cards may be displayed at the same time.
Step 3 — Sing & Move: The teacher opens the Move with the Weather! panel and plays the accompaniment. Pupils sing: Sunny, sunny, touch the sky; Rainy, rainy, hands down low; Windy, windy, sway around; Cloudy, cloudy, turn around.
Step 4 — Weather Riddle Challenge: Pupils listen to or read each riddle, predict the weather word, then flip the riddle card to reveal the answer. The teacher uses Next Riddle to continue.

Suggested assessment: Observe whether pupils can identify the four target words, pronounce them understandably and perform the matching action.

🔍
Activity 2

5.2 Weather Word Hunt

Focus: Reading & Phonics

Step 1 — Shared Poetry Reading: Display Robert Louis Stevenson’s Rain. Pupils follow the text while the teacher reads it or activates Listen to Poem (Gentle Recital).
Step 2 — Explore Key Words: Pupils tap highlighted words such as rain, raining, field, tree, umbrellas, ships and sea. The website displays a short explanation and provides a pronunciation button.
Step 3 — Rhyme Match Challenge: Pupils listen to the ending sound in tree and choose its rhyming partner. The correct match is tree–sea. The website also offers bee and see as additional /iː/ examples.
Step 4 — Physical Extension: Pupils complete the printed Weather Word Hunt worksheet or match physical rhyme cards in pairs.

Suggested assessment: Check whether pupils can identify relevant poem words and recognise tree–sea as a rhyming pair.

🎨
Activity 3

5.3 Draw It, Write It! Studio

Focus: Writing, Drawing & Speaking

Step 1 — Choose Words: Pupils use the Interactive Word Bank. Weather choices include sunny, rainy, windy, cloudy, stormy and snowy. Descriptive choices include bright, grey, warm, cold, strong and soft. Feeling choices include cheerful, happy, calm, excited and peaceful.
Step 2 — Build a Four-Line Poem: Pupils complete the guided lines:
1. Today the weather is ______.
2. The ______ sky is above me.
3. I can see ______.
4. I feel ______ today.
Step 3 — Draw: Pupils create an illustration using the built-in drawing tools. They may select a colour, choose Fine / Normal / Bold stroke size, use the eraser, clear the canvas, or open the Fullscreen Drawing Studio.
Step 4 — Save Fullscreen Drawing: In fullscreen mode, pupils draw with more space and click Save & Exit Fullscreen to return the artwork to the main activity.
Step 5 — Recite: Pupils select Recite My Poem Aloud! to hear their completed poem. The teacher may then invite pupils to perform their poems orally.
Step 6 — Export: Select Save My Poem & Drawing (Download PNG) to create a downloadable illustrated poem certificate.

Suggested assessment: Check that the pupil produces four lines, uses appropriate descriptive language and can share the poem orally.

5.4 Closure

Review the four core weather words, ask pupils to recall the tree–sea rhyme, and use the physical Exit Ticket or a short oral reflection. Pupils may briefly share one word or idea they learned from the lesson.

6.0 Interactive Features Operational Guide

6.1 Background Music (BGM)

The BGM control is located in the top navigation area on larger screens. Select BGM to start or pause the background audio. Use the adjacent slider to change the volume from low to high. Keep the music soft enough for pupils to hear instructions and speech playback clearly.

6.2 Speech Synthesis

Speech synthesis is used for weather-word pronunciation, weather-card sentences, song lyrics, riddles, the Stevenson poem, rhyme explanations and the pupil-created poem. Selecting a speaking button while another item is playing will stop the previous speech before the new speech begins.

6.3 Interactive Flip Cards

Tap a weather card to rotate it and display an example sentence and movement cue. Use Listen for the word and Read Sentence for the card explanation. Tap the card again to return to the front.

6.4 Weather Riddle Cards

Read or play the riddle, ask pupils to predict the answer, then tap the card to reveal it. Select Next Riddle for another item.

6.5 Interactive Poem Vocabulary

In Activity 2, highlighted poem words are clickable. Selecting a word opens a short meaning prompt. Use Pronounce to hear the selected word.

6.6 Rhyme Match

Pupils select one of the available options to match the target word tree. Correct and incorrect choices provide immediate visual feedback. Hear Why They Rhyme provides an audio explanation.

6.7 Drawing Tools

Pupils can choose from the available colours, change the brush size, activate the eraser, clear the drawing, or use fullscreen mode. For best results on touch devices, pupils should draw slowly and avoid scrolling while drawing.

6.8 PNG Export

The website can generate a PNG containing the pupil’s poem and illustration. The browser must allow file downloads. Teachers should check the device’s Downloads folder after the export is completed.

9.0 Teacher Zone

Open Teacher Zone from the upper-right navigation area. The panel provides an English speech-synthesis voice selector and a View User Manual button.

The website automatically filters available English voices on the device where possible. The selected speech output uses a slower classroom-friendly rate and slightly raised pitch. Because the available voice names depend on the operating system and browser, the exact list may differ from one device to another.

User Manual link: the website expects this file to be named Manual_Pengguna.html and stored in the same folder as index.html.

11.0 Troubleshooting

IssueSuggested Solution
No speech playbackCheck device volume, confirm that the browser supports speech synthesis, open Teacher Zone and select another available English voice, then use Test Voice.
Voice sounds different on another computerThis is normal. System voices vary by operating system and browser.
BGM is too loudReduce the BGM volume slider or pause BGM during spoken instructions.
Images or icons are missingConfirm that all PNG assets are in the correct folder and that the internet connection is available for fallback icons.
Drawing does not respondTry the mouse/touchpad, refresh the page, or reopen Activity 3. On touch devices, avoid accidental page scrolling while drawing.
Fullscreen artwork does not returnUse Save & Exit Fullscreen rather than closing the browser tab.
PNG does not downloadCheck browser download permissions and the device’s Downloads folder.
User Manual button does not openConfirm that Manual_Pengguna.html is uploaded beside index.html and that the file name matches exactly.
Website layout is too smallUse browser fullscreen mode, increase zoom, or project the page onto a larger display.

12.0 Classroom Use & Accessibility Tips

  • Keep audio at a safe and comfortable classroom volume.
  • Pause BGM whenever pupils need to focus on pronunciation or teacher instructions.
  • Allow pupils who are uncomfortable performing alone to sing or recite with a partner or group.
  • Use the physical word bank and poem template for pupils who need additional writing support.
  • Allow more proficient pupils to replace the sentence frames with their own lines or add simple rhyme.
  • Use printed materials as a backup if internet, audio or display equipment fails.
